# Nocodelytics — the project story

> Nocodelytics (nocodelytics.com) was an analytics platform for Webflow sites.
> Florian Herrengt co-founded it in June 2021, ran all engineering, and grew it
> to one of the most-installed analytics apps on the Webflow Marketplace. The
> company was acquired in October 2024.

Updated: 2026-11-14. Companion to https://florianherrengt.com/llms.txt.

## The short version

- Co-founded June 2021; acquired October 2024; Florian stayed through the
  handover until December 2024.
- More than 1,000 Webflow sites tracked; the marketplace listing later
  reached the 5,000+ installs category.
- Processed hundreds of millions of events at hundreds of requests per
  second — nowhere near the architecture's limits, which could take
  significantly more.
- Among the first applications on the Webflow Marketplace, featured in the
  Webflow Conf 2022 keynote.
- Florian's roles: co-founder, head of engineering, and — for the final 16
  months — the entire company: product, platform, operations and support.

## The product

Analytics built specifically for Webflow. One-click install through Webflow
OAuth; no code, no tag manager, no GDPR banner required.

- Clicks, links, forms, searches and page views, tracked per element.
- CMS analytics: which Webflow collection items and list entries people
  engaged with — a category no other analytics tool covered.
- Drag-and-build dashboards, shareable and embeddable on client sites.
- Visitor-level journeys, membership analytics (identified visitors via
  Memberstack and Outseta), CSV export, live feed.
- Privacy-first positioning: "Your subscription funds us, not your data."
  GDPR self-service page, DPA, EU-aware consent handling, bot and
  datacenter-IP filtering.

## Joining and the rebuild (2021)

Sarwech Shar had built and launched the MVP solo; it had paying customers
within months (including Makerpad). He invited Florian in as co-founder in
June 2021. The announcement post called Florian "an amazing developer" and
set the mission: rebuild the entire platform.

Florian rebuilt the product from scratch as a TypeScript monorepo:

- Webflow OAuth sign-up and deep Webflow API integrations from day one.
- An event pipeline designed for volume: a lightweight tracker script, a
  dedicated ingestion API, durable queues, and columnar storage.
- A React dashboard with GraphQL, typed end to end with code generation.
- The team grew to three (co-founders plus a frontend developer).

## Scale and traction

- Hundreds of millions of events processed across the product's lifetime.
- Hundreds of requests per second at peak, handled with room to spare by a
  pipeline one engineer designed and operated alone.
- Marketplace listing showed 1.6k installs within weeks of the Webflow
  Marketplace launch (November 2022); the listing later reached the 5,000+
  installs analytics category.
- Customers included Makerpad and design agencies (Sweor, Erickson Built,
  253 Media), plus integrations and testimonials from Jetboost's founder
  and NoCode.Tech ("far beyond anything on the market", "truly an
  unfair advantage for our agency").
- Paid product from day one: Stripe subscriptions and native Webflow
  Marketplace billing, six tiers, free plan funnel.

## Engineering decisions worth knowing

Every piece of the stack was chosen by measuring, in production, by one
person:

- **Storage migration.** Events started in TimescaleDB. Florian's wide-table
  layout cut query cost by up to 6x — work notable enough that Timescale
  published the write-up on their own engineering blog. When scale demanded
  it, event storage moved to ClickHouse in 2023, with PostgreSQL (behind
  PgBouncer) for relational data.
- **Pipeline.** Ingestion API on Fastify, NATS JetStream as the durable
  queue, workers for geo-IP, device parsing and enrichment, batched
  async inserts into ClickHouse. Failed events persisted to a table and
  replayed, never dropped.
- **Benchmarked alternatives, then chose simplicity.** A Go rewrite of the
  ingestion API (working, benchmarked), an AWS Kinesis/Glue/Athena
  serverless pipeline (built as code, costed, rejected), AWS Amplify
  (prototyped). The self-hosted pipeline won on cost and operability.
- **Infrastructure.** Heroku, AWS, Kubernetes and DigitalOcean were each
  tried; the final platform ran on a single self-managed k3s cluster:
  Longhorn storage, Velero backups, Traefik, kustomize staging/production
  overlays, self-hosted Gitea, Drone CI and a private container registry.
  Florian was the platform team.
- **Webflow integration depth.** Support for very large CMS collections —
  sites with 10,000+ items — which was hard to build and one of the
  product's selling points. CMS event attribution solved without any
  Webflow hooks, by parsing rendered pages to infer which collection and
  item a click belonged to.
- **Designer extension.** When Webflow opened Designer extensions in 2023,
  Florian shipped one in about two weeks: views and visitor charts for the
  page being edited, inside Webflow's own tool.
- **Quality bar.** 1,400+ commits over four years, written by one person
  in a two-person company — before AI assistants, when every commit was
  hand-written work; 125 test files; end-to-end tests that verify events
  land in ClickHouse, run against live staging on every deploy; SQL
  migrations applied automatically in pipelines; a scheduled production
  data-integrity check ("events inserted in the last 2h") with email
  alerts; daily encrypted backups (Velero, ClickHouse BACKUP to S3,
  pg_dump off-site).

## Timeline

- 2020: MVP built and launched by Sarwech; paying customers within months.
- April 2021: NOCODELYTICS LTD incorporated in London.
- June 2021: Florian joins as co-founder; full platform rebuild begins.
- November 2022: Webflow Marketplace launch; Webflow Conf keynote feature;
  1.6k installs within weeks; Timescale guest post and interview.
- 2023: Events moved to ClickHouse; tracker migrated to Fastify; Webflow
  Designer extension shipped. From mid-2023, Florian ran the company alone.
- October 2024: Nocodelytics acquired. Florian stayed through the handover
  until December 2024.

## What it demonstrates

- Shipped and sold a real SaaS: product, pricing, billing, support,
  compliance, and an exit — as a founder, not an employee.
- Data engineering at meaningful scale: hundreds of millions of events,
  hundreds of requests per second, on a budget a bootstrapped founder could
  afford.
- Judgment: measured every "obvious" technology (Go, Kinesis, Athena,
  managed Kubernetes) against reality and kept what actually worked.
- End-to-end ownership: code, infrastructure, CI/CD, observability,
  backups, security and GDPR — one person, four years, no incidents that
  took the product down. Stability matters more in analytics than in most
  products: when the tracker is down, events are missed forever.
